Division I — The FMS Diagnostic Method

Logic over intuition.
Sequence over speed.

Every technician follows the same six-step sequence on every visit — developed from thirteen years of field experience to eliminate callbacks and ensure no variable is skipped or assumed.

IPower
IIAirflow
IIIElectrical
IVRefrigerant
VControls
VIEnvironment
Step I

Power Verification

Voltage confirmed at source and load. Disconnects, fuses, and breaker behavior verified directly. Assumed power is a liability. Confirmed power is a foundation.

Step II

Airflow Assessment

Filter condition, blower response, and static pressure evaluated before any refrigerant analysis. Airflow problems misread as refrigerant problems are the most common source of repeat service calls.

Step III

Electrical Systems

Capacitance, contactor integrity, wiring condition, relay function, and amp draw measured under live load. Electrical faults that pass a visual inspection are caught here.

Step IV

Refrigerant Analysis

Superheat and subcool measured after airflow is confirmed — not pressure alone. Correct refrigerant charge readings require correct airflow first. Sequence matters.

Step V

Controls Verification

Thermostat logic, communication voltage, sensor accuracy, staging, and safety interlocks all verified. Control failures are frequently misattributed to mechanical faults.

Step VI

Environmental Factors

Rooftop load, ambient interference, drainage slope, and site-specific humidity conditions reviewed. Systems that pass all prior steps and still underperform have an environmental answer.
